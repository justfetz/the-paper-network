What a unilateral $70/t increase does to open-market volume

Integrated tonnage does not move: a company's own box plants keep buying from its own mills. The open-market tons are the ones that can walk, and in a market where the next mill is $5/t away, they all do. Real increases hold because the producers announce them together and the index follows.

The model

Each mill is a source with a capacity and a set of grades. Each demand point wants a fixed number of tons of liner and of medium. Moving a ton costs freight, which rises with distance and switches from truck to rail near 450 road miles. Minimize freight subject to meeting demand without exceeding any mill: a transportation problem, the special case of min-cost flow where every arc runs from a source to a sink. Two things about this industry break the plain version, and both are fixed by adding nodes rather than changing the algorithm.

First, the big companies are integrated: a ton of IP board goes to the nearest IP plant. Each integrated owner gets its own copy of every demand point holding its share of the local market (IP 31%, Smurfit Westrock 20%, PCA 15.5%, Georgia-Pacific 9.5%, Pratt 6%, Green Bay 3%, Cascades 2%; 13% independents), served only by its own mills; an arc from the open market into each copy at $150/t lets an owner buy outside when short. Second, mills make specific grades: splitting each mill into a liner node and a medium node behind one capacity arc keeps kraft mills out of the medium market without a second commodity. With those expansions it is one min-cost flow, solved as an LP by HiGHS.

# sets: mills i (capacity c_i, grades), demand points j, grades g ∈ {liner, medium}, owners k, ports p
min Σ f_ij (x_ijg + y_ijg) + Σ f_ip z_ip + 150 Σ v_kjg + 400 Σ u_jg
s.t. Σ_{j,g} (x_ijg + y_ijg) + Σ_p z_ip ≤ c_i a_i # mill capacity, a_i = availability this month
Σ_{i owned by k} x_ijg + v_kjg = s_k φ_g D_j # owner k's box plants at j
Σ_i y_ijg − Σ_k v_kjg + u_jg = s_open φ_g D_j # open market at j
Σ_i z_ip = E_p # exports through gateway p (kraft mills only)
f(d) = min(8 + 0.125 d·ft, 35 + 0.065 d·fr) $/ton, d = 1.2 × great-circle miles, ft/fr = diesel factors (22% / 12% fuel share at $3.75)
# October 2026 instance: 93,423 variables, 7,139 constraints, 2.3 s with HiGHS. 55 scenarios on this page.

Duals. The multiplier on a mill's capacity row is what one more ton of that mill saves the network (the scarcity layer). The multiplier on a demand row is the delivered cost of the marginal ton in that county (the delivered-cost layer and the calculator's freight). Prospects rank, for every market, the active mills by delivered freight; the gap between the first two is the switching threshold. Price shocks add $70/t to one owner's open-market arcs only. The fiber loop is a second transportation LP: counties supply recovered OCC (92% of consumption, plus 3% for imported goods), recycled mills demand 1.15 t per ton of recycled board they ship, export docks take the AF&PA/USITC export tonnage, and freight decides. The maintenance calendar is an assignment problem: for each mill and month the cost of an outage is estimated as capacity dual × tons lost, from twelve no-outage solves, then assigned with at most 9 mills per month and two per owner (a small MILP), and re-solved month by month to measure the true penalty. Knockouts remove one mill and re-solve. The backtest puts 2026 demand on the 2024 mill set and asks which mills the LP leaves idle.

Assumptions that matter

  • Demand geography is population tilted by state manufacturing share, rolled up to 441 points; two thirds liner, one third medium; 34.5 Mt domestic + 4.6 Mt exports in 2024, 32.2 + 3.6 in 2026. Canadian mills sell 35% of capacity south; Mexican mills are listed, not modeled.
  • Owner shares of the box market are national; a state-level plant count by owner (in the 10-K property lists) would make them regional.
  • Mill capacities are public figures of mixed vintage; owner totals reconcile to the 10-Ks, single IP or PCA legacy mills could be off by 10%.
  • Freight is two straight lines with a diesel factor. No lanes, backhaul, car supply, transload economics or contracts; no swaps beyond the $150/t outside-purchase arc.
  • The outage calendar (9 days kraft, 5 recycled, 3% unplanned) and the monthly demand index are the author's; companies' actual outage months are in their earnings guidance.

The core of the LP, in Python

owners = [k for k in owner_shares if (M.owner==k).any()]
s_open = 1.0 - sum(owner_shares[k] for k in owners)
grades = [("L", grade_split[0], liner_ok), ("M", grade_split[1], medium_ok)]
# ---- variable bookkeeping
cols = []  # (kind, k, m, j, g) ; kind in x,y,v,z,u
c_obj = []
rows_cap, rows_own, rows_open, rows_exp = [], {}, {}, []
A_r, A_c, A_v = [], [], []
def addvar(kind, k, m, j, g, c):
    cols.append((kind, k, m, j, g)); c_obj.append(c); return len(cols)-1
# constraint indices
cap_row = {m: m for m in range(nM)}
nrow = nM
own_row = {}
for k in owners:
    for j in range(J):
        for gi,(g,_,_) in enumerate(grades):
            own_row[(k,j,g)] = nrow; nrow += 1
open_row = {}
for j in range(J):
    for g,_,_ in grades:
        open_row[(j,g)] = nrow; nrow += 1
exp_row = {p: nrow+p for p in range(len(PORTS))}; nrow += len(PORTS)
def coef(r, cidx, v): A_r.append(r); A_c.append(cidx); A_v.append(v)
owner_of = M.owner.values
for m in range(nM):
    if cap[m] <= 0: continue
    for g, phi, ok in grades:
        if not ok[m]: continue
        for j in range(J):
            # open-market arc
            ci = addvar("y", None, m, j, g, cost[m,j] + surcharge[m]); coef(cap_row[m], ci, 1); coef(open_row[(j,g)], ci, 1)
            k = owner_of[m]
            if k in owner_shares and k in owners:
                ci = addvar("x", k, m, j, g, cost[m,j]); coef(cap_row[m], ci, 1); coef(own_row[(k,j,g)], ci, 1)
    if kraft[m]:
        for p in range(len(PORTS)):
            ci = addvar("z", None, m, None, p, pcost[m,p]); coef(cap_row[m], ci, 1); coef(exp_row[p], ci, 1)
for k in owners:
    for j in range(J):
        for g,_,_ in grades:
            ci = addvar("v", k, None, j, g, premium); coef(own_row[(k,j,g)], ci, 1); coef(open_row[(j,g)], ci, -1)
for j in range(J):
    for g,_,_ in grades:
        ci = addvar("u", None, None, j, g, unmet_penalty); coef(open_row[(j,g)], ci, 1)
nvar = len(cols)
A = coo_matrix((A_v, (A_r, A_c)), shape=(nrow, nvar)).tocsr()
# split into inequality (capacity rows) and equality rows
A_ub = A[:nM]; b_ub = cap
A_eq = A[nM:]
b_eq = np.zeros(nrow-nM)
for k in owners:
    for j in range(J):
        for g, phi, _ in grades:
            b_eq[own_row[(k,j,g)]-nM] = owner_shares[k]*phi*D[j]
for j in range(J):
    for g, phi, _ in grades:
        b_eq[open_row[(j,g)]-nM] = s_open*phi*D[j]
for p in range(len(PORTS)):
    b_eq[exp_row[p]-nM] = export_tons*PORTS.share.values[p]
res = linprog(np.array(c_obj), A_ub=A_ub, b_ub=b_ub, A_eq=A_eq, b_eq=b_eq, bounds=(0,None), method="highs")

What the next version needs

  • EPA Facility Registry Service bulk CSV, NAICS 322211: the free list of every US box plant with coordinates. Replaces the demand proxy. No key.
  • Census County Business Patterns key (free): plants and employment by county, to weight demand and check the list.
  • Google Places key (~$50–100 one-time) or Overpass (free): plant discovery and contact details for the call sheet.
  • EIA key (free): weekly diesel by region, live into the freight factor.
  • Fastmarkets RISI (paid): the actual price series, East and West; AF&PA and FBA reports (members): mill-level grades, regional and end-use shipments.
  • AICC/FBA directories and a job-posting feed: ground truth for independents and a cheap corrugator-vs-sheet-plant tag.
